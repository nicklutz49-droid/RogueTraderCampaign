# WH40K FIDELITY PRIMER — GM CRIB SHEET

> **Canon status:** Setting reference, not campaign canon. Nothing here is a new fact about the Reach; it is how the Warhammer 40,000 universe works, plus this campaign's standing house rules. State files record what the player knows.

Use this when play touches Imperial machinery, void travel, or diction. For campaign-specific canon, follow the cross-references — this file invents nothing.

## Warp travel — the short version

- Ships cross interstellar distances by translating into the **Immaterium** (the warp): an ocean of raw psychic energy where time and distance behave badly. Real-space weeks can pass as warp-days, or vice versa. Never promise exact arrival times; captains speak in estimates and prayers.
- **Typical times:** days between neighboring systems on a calm, charted route; **weeks** for a subsector crossing; **months** for sector-spanning voyages. Uncharted or stormy routes multiply everything. The Vortigern Passage transit took two hard weeks (state/campaign_log.md, Entry 001).
- **Warp storms** can seal routes for centuries — the Passage was shut for two millennia (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md). Treat storms as weather with malice.
- Translation in/out happens at a system's edge, past the gravity well. In-system travel is real-space plasma burn: hours to days between planets. Auspex returns crawl at lightspeed — what you see of the outer system is old news.

## Geller fields

- The Geller field is the bubble of real-space that keeps a ship's crew sane and un-eaten while in the warp. It flickering is the worst sound a voidsman knows.
- A failing field means intrusion: whispers, nightmares, crew madness, and — in true failure — things coming aboard. The Passage transit's repeated Geller alarms were genuinely dangerous, and the crew knows it.
- Field integrity is the Enginseers' most sacred charge. Rituals, unguents, and incense are not superstition; in the Imperium they *work*, or at least nobody dares find out otherwise.

## Navigators (Navis Nobilite)

- Mutant-noble bloodlines bearing the **third eye** (warp eye), the only means of steering a ship through the warp over real distances by sighting the **Astronomican** — the psychic lighthouse of Terra.
- Organized into Great Houses, jealous of charts and contracts. A Navigator is aboard by *contract with their House*, not personal fealty; they answer to their Novator first. See lore/factions/house_vor_kaas.md and lore/people/ilyana_vor_kaas.md.
- Etiquette: never look at the third eye (its gaze kills), never touch the bandana or hood, address as "Navigator" or by honorific. A ship without one is trapped at sublight — the Navigator is irreplaceable and everyone aboard knows it.
- Ranks in a house run roughly Novator (master of the house) > Navigator Primus > Secundus > Tertius. Ilyana's Secundus posting at 26 is notably young.

## Astropaths — and THIS campaign's muffled-astropathy rule

- Interstellar communication is telepathy: **astropaths**, sanctioned psykers soul-bound to the Emperor (usually blinded by the rite), sending dream-symbol messages between choirs. Range is limited; long messages relay station to station and arrive as *interpretations*, not transcripts.
- Normal expectations: days to weeks for a message to cross a sector, with distortion. There is no such thing as a quick, clear interstellar call.
- **CAMPAIGN RULE (LOCKED, per lore/places/hesperus_reach.md):** the Reach's warp is shallow and becalmed. **Astropathy is muffled** — sendings inside the Reach are short-ranged; traffic to/from Calixis takes **weeks** and arrives **garbled and partial**. Nobody phones home quickly: not the player, not his rivals, not the spies aboard. Every off-ship schemer runs on stale information. Enforce this ruthlessly; it is a pillar of the campaign.
- Astropath Transcendant Maddalen Rhee and her small choir aboard the *Intemperance* are the dynasty's only voice to the Imperium (lore/dynasty/fleet.md).

## Throne Gelt & Imperial economics

- The Imperium's notional currency is the **Throne Gelt** ("Thrones"), but at Rogue Trader scale wealth is *capability*: hulls, cargo, charts, contracts, tithes, favors, and the abstract **Profit Factor** tracked in state/dynasty_ledger.md.
- Quote Thrones for personal-scale sums (bribes, bar tabs, a mercenary's fee). Anything dynasty-scale moves the Profit Factor meter instead. Barter, salvage rights, and monopoly charters matter more than coin in a frontier like the Reach.

## Warrants of Trade — the legalities

- A Warrant of Trade is a hereditary charter from the highest Imperial authority granting rights of trade, conquest, and colonization **beyond the Imperium's borders**. Within Imperial space a Rogue Trader is a citizen (a grand one); beyond the Emperor's light, the Warrant *is* the law — its holder may treat with xenos, claim worlds, raise armies, and dispense justice.
- Warrants vary wildly in wording. Old ones (like the Vharadan Warrant, 809.M38) tend to be archaic and generous — and archaic wording cuts both ways in an Administratum audit. The Vharadan Warrant's clauses are a lawyer's goldmine (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md).
- Succession disputes are settled by Imperial courts, precedent, and force of possession. Aurelian's seizure was legal — *mostly* — which is exactly the seam the displaced line's case picks at (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md).
- Key GM lever: in the Reach, the Warrant makes Aurelian sovereign. The moment the Imperium's institutions arrive in force (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md), "sovereign" becomes "negotiable."

## Chaos-as-whispers doctrine (per CLAUDE.md)

- Chaos in this campaign **whispers; it does not announce**. No daemon hosts, no cackling cultists in Act One. Corruption is a long game played through ambition, resentment, and "one necessary compromise."
- Aurelian's drive — raw power, never be weak — is precisely the tinder the Ruinous Powers favor. Plant small temptations: a shortcut, a weapon, a voice in a Geller-alarm dream. **Never corrupt the player without his choices leading there.**
- Reinforcing rule: daemonic incursion in the Reach is **rare and weak** (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md — the becalmed warp). This is a feature: it keeps Chaos to whispers mechanically, not just tonally. Why the warp is becalmed is a deep secret (lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md).

## Original-xenos policy for the Reach

- The galaxy's canon xenos (Orks, Eldar, Drukhari, Tyranids, Necrons, etc.) all exist and may be *referenced* — in histories, Cole's war stories, Guard slang, Cold Trade rumor.
- **Everything actually encountered IN the Reach stays original to this campaign.** No canon xenos species on-screen here. The region's alien material is the campaign's own: the extinct builders of the Hesperus Prime IV ruin and whatever else the GM grows within 40k logic.
- Rationale: the Reach was sealed for two millennia and is the campaign's sandbox; original threats keep every discovery genuinely unknown to player *and* character.

## GM secrets

- The primer itself contains no unrevealed facts, but three campaign rules above touch deep canon; know the sources before improvising near them:
  - The **becalmed warp / muffled astropathy** has a physical cause: the Karyx lattice at Hesperus Prime IV (lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md). The Passage's stability is tied to it. Never explain the muffling in play until the lattice canon surfaces.
  - The **Ordo Hereticus** row is live: Marguerite reports to Inquisitor Hadria Veck via coded sermon-digests — which the muffled-astropathy rule slows to a crawl (lore/people/sister_marguerite.md). Veck's picture of Aurelian will always be months stale.
  - The **Chaos-as-whispers** doctrine has a long-game exception on a very slow fuse: the Becalmed (lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md). Never rush it on-screen.

## Hooks

1. **A garbled sending arrives.** Rhee delivers a weeks-old, half-legible message from Calixis — a creditor's demand, a family threat, a warning — and the player must act on fragments. Demonstrates the muffled-astropathy rule in play.
2. **Warrant lawyering.** A rival (Locke, later Baltrane — lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md, lore/factions/imperial_actors.md) disputes a salvage or planetary claim; resolution turns on the Vharadan Warrant's archaic clauses, read aloud, meteoric-iron seals and all.
3. **Navy vs Guard aboard.** A crisis forces Cole's void-instincts against Aurelian's ground-war instincts in front of the crew — the ancient institutional rivalry made personal, and a chance to define command culture on the *Intemperance*.
4. **The first whisper.** During a Geller alarm or a null-zone brush, something offers Aurelian exactly what the Hunger wants, phrased as his own thought. Small, deniable, declinable. Log whether he lingers on it.
5. **Diction as diplomacy.** First contact with Veyra's nations (lore/places/hesperus_prime_ii_veyra.md) hinges on register: Sister Marguerite can render Imperial ceremony into the colonists' drifted Low Gothic — or the player can botch it and sound like a sky-daemon.
