# HESPERUS PRIME II — "VEYRA"

> **Canon status:** GM reference — not yet revealed in play. State files record what the player knows.

The lost world. A Terran-standard garden world, second orbit of Hesperus Prime,
inhabited by **~38 million souls** who have never heard the God-Emperor's name.
From orbit: green continents, an island-strewn ocean, rail lines like stitching,
smoke over the industrial north, and — on auspex — the muzzle-flash shimmer of
a continent-spanning war.

**This file DECIDES the "not yet established" items in `state/world_state.md`**
(nations, tech level, culture, religion). All of it is now fixed canon,
unrevealed in play. System context: `lore/places/hesperus_prime_system.md`.

| Fact | Canon |
|---|---|

## Origins: the Veyra Dawn

The ark **Veyra Dawn** made planetfall in the Dark Age of Technology and her
colonists never left. Fifteen millennia of collapse, dark centuries, plagues,
petty empires, and slow rebuilding ground the ark itself out of history and
into scripture. The Veyrans know they came from the sky — every creed, king,
and schoolbook agrees on that much — but the how and the who have rotted into
myth. No orbital debris survives; whatever the ark shed in orbit fell or
drifted away millennia ago. To the Veyrans, the stars are ancestral country.

## Language and contact

Veyric will defeat a vox-operator and embarrass a servitor-translator: vowels
have wandered, half the vocabulary is agricultural or liturgical drift, and
formal speech is thick with Church idiom ("by the Fathers' wake" as an oath;
"dawnward" meaning both east and holy). **Sister Marguerite can work with it**
— the Orders Dialogous trained her on exactly this kind of lost-colony drift —
and within days of exposure she can interpret; within weeks, pass. Without
her, first contact runs on gesture, gunfire, or worse
(`lore/people/sister_marguerite.md`).

Crackle-vox traffic is plentiful and unencrypted by void-age standards. A
starship's sensorium can drink the whole planet's signals; making *sense* of
them is the bottleneck.

## What their armies can do

Guard-standard doctrine translates: trenches, wire, rail logistics, massed
artillery, dirigible reconnaissance, and the first crude biplane squadrons.
Chemical shells are the new atrocity — Bray fields them; Tessene answers where
it can. Against void-capable forces they have nothing: no orbital awareness,
no anti-air worth the name above dirigible altitude, no counter to a lance
strike or a drop assault. **But 38 million people cannot be garrisoned by
2,000 house troops.** Veyra can only be taken by politics, faith, or proxy —
which is exactly what makes it interesting.

## The three powers

### The Concordat of Bray — the furnace

- **Character:** the largest power; an industrialized corporate-parliamentary
  oligarchy. Combine-houses hold the franchise; the Directorate chair rotates
  among them. Secular, contractual, ruthless in an accountant's way.
- **Strengths:** sheer output. Foundries, rail net, conscript mass, chemical
  industry. Bray does not out-fight Tessene; it out-produces it, and is
  grinding the Kingdom down year by year.
- **Desperation:** fuel and ore. Eleven years of total war have gutted Bray's
  coalfields and mines; the Directorate's war-solvency arithmetic fails within
  a few more years. They need Tessene's mountains — that is what the Grey War
  is *for* — or a new source no one on Veyra can imagine. A ship that can
  deliver refined fuel and metals owns the Directorate's attention instantly.
- **A starship, to Bray:** a supplier, a technology, a threat — anything but
  a god. The oligarchs will try to *contract* with the sky.

### The Kingdom of Tessene — the mountain

- **Character:** a martial monarchy of the highlands around the **Carven
  Range**. Honor culture, hereditary officer clans, the finest soldiers on
  Veyra — mountain troops Bray's conscripts genuinely fear. The crown's
  ancient style is **the Deepward King**, "warden of what lies beneath."
- **Strengths:** soldiers, terrain, faith. Man for man unbeatable; the Church
  of the Return is strongest here and the war is preached as holy endurance.
- **Desperation:** attrition. Tessene is losing — slowly, gloriously,
  arithmetically. A generation of officer clans lies dead in the Grey
  Marches; the harvests are worked by widows. The court knows the Kingdom has
  perhaps three to five years before collapse. **Tessene is desperate enough
  to bargain with gods from the sky** — and its creed says the gods are due.
- **A starship, to Tessene:** the prophecy, arriving. The Deepward King's
  court will split between rapture and terror, but they will *deal*.

### The Shorelord Compact — the tide

- **Character:** a federation of coastal merchant city-states — the Nine
  Harbours, first among them **Cantlemere** — each under its hereditary
  Shorelord, bound by treaty and mutual suspicion. Officially neutral in the
  Grey War; actually its bankers, smugglers, and arms-brokers.
- **Strengths:** the only real navy on Veyra, the merchant fleets, the
  exchanges. Both belligerents float on Compact credit and Compact hulls.
- **Desperation:** the ending. Neutral profiteering only pays while the war
  lasts and stays balanced. If Bray wins outright, the Directorate's combines
  swallow the Harbours; if Tessene collapses too fast, the debts die with it.
  The Compact needs the war *managed* — and a new power from the sky wrecks
  every ledger they keep.
- **A starship, to the Compact:** a market and a menace. The Shorelords will
  be first to offer harbor, trade, and flattery — and first to sell
  information about the visitors to anyone paying.

## The Grey War (Year 11)

Named for the mud, the gas-haze, and the uniforms of both sides in the
**Grey Marches** — the ruined front country between Bray's lowlands and
Tessene's foothills. Eleven years of trench attrition: Bray attacks with
shells and gas and conscript mass; Tessene bleeds it white on the slopes and
counterattacks with mountain troops by night. The front has moved less than a
hundred kilometers in a decade. The Compact ships to both shores.

Everyone on Veyra understands the shape of the ending: Bray wins in three to
five years unless something changes. **The flotilla's arrival is the
something.** Whichever power the sky favors — or seems to — wins; the GM
should treat every Vharadan action in-system as a stone dropped in this pond.

## The Church of the Return

The unifying creed of Veyra, older than any nation: the **Sky Fathers** — the
ark-crew, garbled into divinity across fifteen millennia — departed into the
heavens and **will return**, to judge the unworthy and gather the faithful.
Its scripture, the **Book of Landings**, is corrupted ark-memory: litanies
that scan suspiciously like status liturgies and evacuation drills, saints
with duty-roster names. Doctrine differs on whether the Return brings mercy
or fire; every congregation carries both verses.

| Power | The Church's standing | When a ship descends |
|---|---|---|
| **Tessene** | State faith, strongest here; the crown holds high religious office | Prophecy fulfilled. Rapture, dread, and immediate theological politics — is the visitor Father, herald, or deceiver? |
| **Bray** | Suppressed but simmering; the oligarchy disestablished the Church generations ago, and the industrial poor still believe | A crisis. Millennial fervor in the tenement rows; the Directorate caught between crackdown and panic |
| **Compact** | Syncretic and easygoing — the Fathers share altars with sea-luck spirits and ancestor shrines | An omen to be read profitably. The Shorelords' priests will bless whatever the ship turns out to be |

**A starship descending from orbit walks straight into this prophecy.** There
is no neutral arrival: land in Tessene and be hailed; land in Bray and ignite
its underclass; land in the Harbours and be appraised. And Arch-Confessor
Jubal Kest is months behind the flotilla — the Missionaria will find the
Church of the Return either a miracle of providence or a heresy to burn,
depending entirely on how it has been handled by then
(`lore/factions/imperial_actors.md`).

## Canon established in play (Days 15–23)

- **Grisholm** — capital of the Concordat of Bray; riverine industrial
  metropolis, seat of the Directorate; tenement rows thick with suppressed
  Church of the Return believers (now risen openly since the Proclamation).
- **The Vantery Arsenal** — Bray's great shell-works, gas-plant, and six-way
  rail junction, 20 km upriver of Grisholm; owned by Combine Kolvax.
  DESTROYED Day 20 by lance strike (the "Vantery Judgment"), ~4,000 dead.
  Now a glass-lipped crater and a theological argument.
- **Combine Kolvax** — Bray armaments/chemicals combine-house, the Grey
  War's chief profiteer; repudiated the Directorate's capitulation rather
  than face peace, audit, and the loss of its arsenal monopoly. Rebellion
  BROKEN Days 31–52 by Aurelian's orbital-mobility campaign ("the Long
  Night": the Rail-Cut, the Greywell gas trap, decapitation raids).
  **Marshal-Director Ansel Kolvax** — third son of the founding line,
  competent, unsentimental about his own house's guilt — surrendered
  28,000 to the Herald's justice alone. The combine's industrial estate
  (arms plants, chemical works, foundries) sits forfeit and contested.
  GM note: the combine's civilian patriarchs fled into Compact banking
  havens with much of the liquid fortune — a future thread.
- **The Greenvault & Dawnholt** — fertile, defensible valley in Tessene's
  eastern foothills ("dawnward" = holy east), war-emptied, granted by the
  Deepward King Day 25. **Dawnholt** founded Day 28: ~5,400 Calixian
  colonists, prefab habs, plasma still, retainer-company garrison under
  Armsmaster Ferro. Already a pilgrimage magnet for the Church of the
  Return — a growing levy/labor pool and a theological flashpoint the
  Missionaria will eventually inspect. GM note: the Greenvault sits in the
  FOOTHILLS of the same range that hides the Deep Shrine (see GM secrets) —
  the dynasty's colony and Veyra's greatest secret share a horizon.
- The **Proclamation of Veyra** (Day 15) and its aftermath are logged in
  `state/campaign_log.md` Entries 004–005; per-power standings live in
  `state/world_state.md`. The Marguerite orthodoxy framing (worship to the
  Emperor; Aurelian as herald/vicar only) is now load-bearing public canon
  on Veyra — Kest's Missionaria will find a planet mid-conversion to a
  *technically orthodox* creed (`lore/factions/imperial_actors.md`).

## GM secrets

- **The Deep Shrine (LOCKED).** Beneath the **Carven Range** in Tessene lies
  the buried **stern-section of the *Veyra Dawn*** — kilometers of Dark Age
  hull entombed by planetfall and fifteen millennia of geology. The Tessene
  crown knows it as the **Deep Shrine**: the holiest place on Veyra, sealed
  vaults tended by hereditary shrine-wardens, entered only at coronations.
  This is why the Deepward King is "warden of what lies beneath" — the title
  is literal and nobody outside the innermost court remembers that.
- **The regalia (LOCKED).** The Tessene royal regalia contain **functional
  archeotech** drawn from the Shrine over the centuries: power cores that
  have burned for millennia (the "Undying Lamps" of the coronation rite), a
  stasis relic preserving something the wardens call the First Father's
  vestment. The court believes them miracles. They are inventory.
- **Mechanicus stakes (LOCKED).** A Dark Age ark — intact hull sections,
  STC-derived systems, live power cores — is a prize the Mechanicus would
  burn worlds for, and 38 million souls would not tip their arithmetic.
  **Vex-Han-9 does not know yet**; his surveys are fixed on Hesperus Prime IV.
  But sustained auspex work on Veyra will eventually itch at him — anomalous
  power signatures under the Carven Range — and if he confirms an ark, his
  encrypted channel to Archmagos Ruuk becomes a countdown
  (`lore/people/vex_han_9.md`, `lore/factions/imperial_actors.md`).
- **Pacing.** Reveal in this order as play allows: prophecy politics first,
  the Deep Shrine's existence second, the regalia's nature third, the ark
  itself last. The Shrine should surface through Tessene diplomacy or
  Marguerite's translation work, not a sensor sweep.

## Hooks

1. **The kingmaker's choice.** Fuel and ore for Bray, or soldiers-from-heaven
   for Tessene, or a quiet arrangement with the Compact — any one of the
   three powers, backed from orbit, wins the Grey War. Each path yields a
   different Veyra: a contracted client, a holy protectorate, or a bought one.
2. **Thirty-eight million recruits.** Veyra is the manpower the dynasty
   utterly lacks — hardened Tessene infantry, Bray's industrial conscripts,
   Compact void-curious sailors. Raising Veyran regiments means picking a
   patron, a faith posture, and a price.
3. **The prophecy problem.** Descend openly and Aurelian *is* the Return —
   with everything that promises and demands. Manage the revelation, exploit
   it, or try to arrive quietly and fail; and remember Kest's pilgrim hulk is
   on the traffic clock.
4. **Marguerite becomes indispensable.** Every negotiation, intercept, and
   scripture passes through the expedition's one Veyric-capable translator —
   handing the Inquisition's instrument a seat at every table
   (`lore/people/sister_marguerite.md`).
5. **The itch under the mountains.** The moment Vharadan augurs or Vex-Han-9's
   surveys linger on the Carven Range, the Deep Shrine clock starts: does the
   Lord-Captain court Tessene for its soldiers knowing what sleeps under
   their holiest ground — and who he must eventually keep it from?
