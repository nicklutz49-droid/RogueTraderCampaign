## The founder and the Warrant (809.M38)

**Lord-Militant Arcturus Vharadan** earned the Warrant the old way: fleet
service in the reconquest wars of late M38, decades of burned rebel ports and
broken xenos corsairs. The grant was countersigned by the Lord Commander of
the Segmentum acting as the Emperor's Regent — hence the family boast, worn
smooth by three millennia of repetition: *"sealed by a long-dead Emperor's
Regent."* Arcturus took it as license, not honor. His household chronicle
records exactly one sentiment about the ceremony: "Now we begin."

## The golden centuries

When the Angevin Crusade carved out the Calixis Sector, the Vharadans moved
in behind the guns — one of the first Warrant houses to plant roots in the
new sector. Early conquest glories gave way to a mercantile ascendancy that
peaked in M40: a dozen void-worthy ships under the black-and-gold, seats on
three subsector trade cartels, dock rights and rents across the Drusus
Marches. The *Intemperance*, commissioned 233.M40 at the height of it, is
the last hull of that fleet still flying Vharadan colors.

## The century of decline (~717.M41 onward)

The rot was not one disaster but a hundred small comforts. Bad ventures
half-funded and abandoned. Holdings mortgaged to cover the interest on older
mortgages. Ships sold, then the docks the ships had berthed at, then the
cartel seats. By 800.M41 the dynasty was a name, a Warrant in a vault, and a
calendar of parties: the main line kept residence at the **Eclipse Court**,
their gallery-house in Hive Sibellus, Scintilla, living off rents and the
Warrant's prestige. Wealth kept flowing — just enough to keep anyone from
having to be good at anything.

## The usurpation of 815.M41

Colonel Aurelian Vharadan, 33rd Tempestus Scions — a distant branch of the
blood, twenty-eight years old, freshly decorated for storming a heretic
stronghold — was invited to the family's victory celebration in his honor.
He watched his hosts for one evening and concluded the Warrant was being
wasted. Within the year it was his. Legal. Mostly.

### The four levers

| Lever | What he did | What it left behind |
|---|---|---|
| **Debts** | Bought the dynasty's paper through proxies until he held the majority of its obligations, then presented himself as sole creditor | A proxy trail; creditors' paper now circulating (some bought up by the main line — see lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md) |
| **Blackmail** | A dossier on Merovech's brother **Casimir Vharadan** and his dealings with a Cold Trade smuggling ring — xenos artefacts moved through family dock rights | The dossier still exists, aboard the *Intemperance* (see GM secrets) |
| **Favors** | Called in Guard and Administratum favors earned at war — a lord-marshal's adjutant here, three tithe-magisters there — to fast-grade the transfer filings | Men who now consider themselves *owed*, and a filing history a hostile advocate could mine |
| **The duel** | Lifeward-Captain **Euric Vane**, the family champion, challenged Aurelian over "an insult" in the Eclipse Court's winter garden. Vane died on Aurelian's chainsword | Nobody speaks of it, because its legality was… arguable (see GM secrets). Armsmaster Ferro grieves his friend still — lore/dynasty/house_troops.md |

The whole engine ran on the main line's softness: no lever would have worked
on a family that still audited its own books, guarded its own secrets, or
fielded a second champion.

## The Warrant itself

A single vellum charter under **meteoric-iron seals**, granting House
Vharadan rights of trade, conquest, and colonization "beyond the Emperor's
light." Its clauses are archaic and generous — drafted in a late-M38 idiom
of near-unlimited frontier license, the kind of language later millennia of
Administratum practice quietly stopped issuing. A lawyer's goldmine, cutting
both ways: broad enough to justify almost anything Aurelian does in the
Reach, and irregular enough that a hostile audit could tie the dynasty in
knots for decades. Aurelian carries it personally (state/character_sheet.md).

## Port Gyre and the departure (817.M41)

When the Vortigern Passage reopened in late 816.M41, Aurelian liquidated
nearly everything the usurpation had won him — rents, the Eclipse Court's
contents, the last Calixis holdings — and assembled the flotilla at **Port
Gyre**, a half-mortgaged Vharadan dock in the Drusus Marches and the
dynasty's last piece of real property. The final asset sales closed with the
mooring clamps still engaged. Dockside gossip had it that the Lord-Captain
sold the office he signed the papers in. He essentially did: Port Gyre's
remaining equity went to provision the *Dutiful Son*. The transit through
the Passage took two weeks and was rough — repeated Geller field alarms —
before translation into the Hesperus Prime system (state/campaign_log.md,
Entry 001, where the campaign opens).

## Heraldry and motto

- **Arms:** a black sun eclipsing a gold one, on fields of black and gold.
- **Motto:** **"What we hold, we keep."** (rendered in High Gothic on the
  Warrant's seal-ribbon).
- Three thousand years have argued over the eclipse. The golden centuries
  read it as prudence shading profit. The declining line read it as the past
  outshining the present, and hung it everywhere. Aurelian's reading, given
  exactly once, to Major Karsh: "The black sun is whoever is holding the
  Warrant."

## The displaced main line (summary)

At the usurpation the main line consisted of **Lord Merovech Vharadan**
(the displaced Warrant-holder — soft, vain, genuinely wounded; not the
danger), the **Dowager Lady Iolanthe Vharadan** (Merovech's grandmother and
the real spine of the family — bitter, patient, resourceful), Casimir (the
blackmailed brother, now doubly ruined), and assorted cousins, dependents,
and sinecure-holders — scattered, humiliated, some quietly relieved.
Iolanthe is the author of the family's response, which is already in motion.
**Full detail, both prongs of the counterstroke, and the agent already
embarked: lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md.**

## GM secrets

- **Why nobody speaks of the duel:** Vane issued the challenge — but over an
  insult Aurelian engineered across three prior encounters, and the duel was
  fought before Vane had been formally released from his lifeward oath. A
  sworn lifeward cannot lawfully hazard his life in private quarrel; a
  hostile advocate could argue Aurelian therefore killed a man legally
  incapable of consenting to the duel — closer to murder of a house officer
  than an affair of honor. Witnesses were few and have been kept
  comfortable. This is a load-bearing stone in Iolanthe's coercion case
  (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md).
- **The Casimir dossier travels with the fleet.** Aurelian keeps the Cold
  Trade blackmail file in his private vault aboard the *Intemperance* —
  insurance, and habit. It documents xenos-artefact smuggling in enough
  detail to interest the Inquisition, and it proves Aurelian *knew* of Cold
  Trade activity and traded on it rather than reporting it. In Ordo
  Hereticus hands (Sister Marguerite — lore/people/sister_marguerite.md) it
  cuts at Aurelian as sharply as at Casimir.
- **The proxy debt trail is not fully buried.** Two of the proxies Aurelian
  used still hold paper and records in Calixis. Iolanthe's financiers are
  hunting them (the "legal-financial siege," lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md).
- **The Warrant's archaic clauses (GM ammunition):** among its late-M38
  generosities are a right of "first claim upon all salvage and derelict
  charter" in territories it opens (a future collision with Lady-Captain
  Locke — lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md) and an exemption from tithe
  upon armies raised "beyond the Emperor's light" (a future collision with
  the Administratum wave — lore/factions/imperial_actors.md). Invoke these
  when dramatically apt; each use is a triumph now and evidence later.

## Hooks

1. **Ferro and the empty scabbard.** Any scene honoring house tradition can
   surface Euric Vane's absence — Armsmaster Ferro's grief is the crack the
   retainers' loyalty runs along, and the crack Cassia Renn hides inside
   (lore/dynasty/house_troops.md).
2. **A clause in the Lord-Captain's favor.** In a dispute over salvage,
   claims, or levies in the Reach, the Warrant's archaic language hands
   Aurelian a legal hammer — and every witness to its use is a future
   deposition for Iolanthe's case.
3. **Garbled word from Calixis.** Weeks after any astropathic traffic
   resumes, Maddalen Rhee delivers a partial sending: Vharadan paper is
   being bought in quantity on Scintilla, buyer unknown (the legal siege
   arriving on-screen).
4. **The dossier as temptation.** A crisis of leverage invites Aurelian to
   use the Casimir file again — against a creditor, an Imperial actor, or
   the main line — putting its existence one careless step from Marguerite's
   notice.
5. **Someone from Port Gyre.** A freebooter or chartist arriving in the
   early Passage wave (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md) holds Vharadan debt
   paper or a grudge from the fire-sale — small claim, awkward timing,
   witnesses watching how the Lord-Captain honors the name's obligations.
