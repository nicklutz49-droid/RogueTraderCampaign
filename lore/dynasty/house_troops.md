# HOUSE TROOPS — THE VHARADAN REGIMENT

> **Canon status:** GM reference — not yet revealed in play. State files record what the player knows.

Two thousand soldiers are the whole of House Vharadan's ground power: every
lasgun, every boot, every soul the dynasty can put on a planet's surface. They
berth aboard the *Dutiful Son* (lore/dynasty/fleet.md), drilled to Guard
standard through the Port Gyre months and the Passage transit — Aurelian is a
Guard colonel before he is anything else, and it shows in the drill book. As
of campaign Day 1 they have fired no shot in the Reach; honour details rotate
to the flagship, and everything else waits in the transport's troop decks,
sharpening.

The regiment is really three armies wearing one uniform: the Scions who
followed the Colonel, the recruits bought at the dockside, and the retainers
who came with the name. Whether it becomes one army is a campaign question.

| Heavy assets | 3 Sentinels, 12 crawlers, a handful of weapons teams | (dispersed) | see Equipment | — |

Chain of command: the Lord-Captain, then Karsh for war-making, with Ferro
holding an older, vaguer household precedence that has never yet been tested
against her commission. Nobody has forced the question. Someone will.

## The Colonel's Own (~180)

Veterans of the 33rd Tempestus Scions who followed Aurelian out of the Guard
— discharges bought, finessed, or fast-graded with the same called-in favors
that greased the Warrant transfer (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md). They are
the finest soldiers in the Reach until someone proves otherwise: carapace
armour, hellguns, grav-chute and hostile-environment qualified, blooded in
the storming that made the Colonel's name.

They call him **the Colonel**. Never "Lord-Captain" — the honorific is
technically insubordinate and completely untouchable, because it means *we
were there*. They hold the flagship honour posts, the Lord-Captain's
lifeward rotation (a post pointedly vacant of retainers since Euric Vane —
see below), and the assault reserve.

### Major Ivetta Karsh

Blunt, scarred — a lasburn seam from jaw to collar she declines to have
smoothed — and the regiment's working commander. Treats Aurelian as the
Colonel first and Lord-Captain a distant second; in private she budgets his
glory like ammunition. She addresses the dynasty's nobility of function with
open impatience and reserves her courtesy for anyone who can strip a hellgun.
Her standing assessment of the regiment, delivered flat: "One good battle in
us. Two if the first one's cheap."

## The line companies (~1,420)

Seven numbered companies of roughly two hundred: Calixis hive stock signed at
recruiting tables in Sibellus' underhive, Guard washouts sweating out old
demerits, and Port Gyre dockhands who took the coin as the mooring clamps
released — the other companies call these last the **Gyre Rats**, and the
Rats have started answering to it with pride. Equipment is mixed-pattern
lasguns from four different forge-lines and flak armour bought by the crate.

They are better than they should be. Aurelian and Karsh ran them through
transit drill in the *Dutiful Son*'s emptied holds — live-fire lanes between
cargo frames, boarding drill in the dark, Scion sergeants seeded one per
company as drill-brutes. Senior of the seven captains is **Dov Harrec**,
ex-Guard sergeant grade-jumped at Port Gyre: unimaginative, iron-reliable,
honest about both. The line companies have no history, no grudges, and no
loyalty older than their contracts — which makes them, oddly, the most
biddable third of the regiment. They will become whatever their first
campaign makes them.

## The retainer companies (~400)

Hereditary Vharadan armsmen in the black-and-gold, mustered as the **Sable
Company** and the **Gilt Company** after the house heraldry. Third-, fourth-,
fifth-generation service: families that guarded Vharadan docks, halls, and
holds through the golden centuries and stayed through the rot. They drill
with antique precision, maintain their house-pattern arms like relics, and
know ceremonies the main line itself had forgotten. Loyal to the *name* of
Vharadan absolutely — to its current holder, not yet. They watch Aurelian
the way the ancestor portraits do.

The pre-departure muster swelled their ranks: retainer families sent kin —
"cousins-of-service," vouched by blood rather than record — to fill out the
companies before Port Gyre. Roughly sixty of the four hundred are such
late-mustered cousins. Ferro vouched for the tradition; nobody audited the
individuals. (See GM secrets.)

### Armsmaster Hallam Ferro

Third-generation Armsmaster, sworn at sixteen, now grey at the temples and
straight as a pike. Honorable to the grain: he transferred the companies'
oath to the Warrant's lawful holder without hesitation, and he will die
before he breaks it. But he is grieving the old order, and above all one man
— **Lifeward-Captain Euric Vane**, the family champion Aurelian killed in the
duel nobody speaks of (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md), was Ferro's friend of
forty years. Vane's sword and empty scabbard hang in the retainer companies'
shrine-locker aboard the *Dutiful Son*, and Ferro cleans the blade himself,
monthly, saying nothing. Every retainer knows it. So does Karsh. So,
presumably, does the Lord-Captain.

## Equipment and shortfalls

The regiment is drilled to Guard standard and equipped to militia standard.
The ledger (state/dynasty_ledger.md) is honest: Military Power — Strained.

| Asset | Holding | Shortfall |
|---|---|---|
| Sentinel walkers | 3, battered — crews have named them *Patience*, *Pity*, and *Spite* | *Spite* runs; the others take turns. Spares must be begged from Vex-Han-9's adepts (lore/people/vex_han_9.md), at a price in favors |
| Utility crawlers | 12 | Cargo mules, not fighting vehicles; armour value of a strong opinion |
| Heavy weapons teams | A handful — heavy stubbers, two lascannon, a section of mortars | No replacement barrels; lascannon charge is flagship-drawn |
| Small-arms supply | Charge packs by the crate | FOUR incompatible lasgun charge standards across the line companies; hellgun cells recharge only from the *Dutiful Son*'s generatoria |
| Armour / artillery / air wing | **None. None. None.** | The dynasty's single greatest ground weakness; any enemy with a gun bigger than a mortar outranges the regiment |

What this force can do: seize and hold a port, a compound, an orbital dock;
escort and dig in the one colonial foothold; win one sharp battle against
comparable infantry — especially with the Lord-Captain leading from the
front. What it cannot do: fight a continental war, contest armour or
airpower, or absorb losses. Every casualty is irreplaceable until the Reach
provides recruits.

## Frictions

- **Scions and retainers do not mess together.** Between them stands a dead
  man. No Scion has said Vane's name in a retainer's hearing; no retainer has
  called Aurelian "Colonel."
- **Karsh and Ferro** maintain a scrupulous, arctic professional courtesy.
  She thinks his companies are honour-guard theatre; he thinks her Scions are
  a hired knife with one loyalty and no roots. Both are partly right.
- **The line companies watch both** and bet, quietly. Harrec runs a book on
  nothing and knows everything; his companies will follow whichever culture
  wins the regiment.

## GM secrets

- **The knife is in the Sable Company.** **Lifeward Cassia Renn** — sworn to
  Dowager Lady Iolanthe, mission and sanctions detailed in
  lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md — embarked at Port Gyre among the
  late-mustered cousins-of-service, on the rolls as **Armswoman Cassia
  Vael**, vouched by a retainer family in the main line's debt. Her tradecraft
  is why it works: Iolanthe recruited her from outside the household
  precisely so no retainer knows her face, and the Lifewards guarded the
  Eclipse Court on Scintilla while Ferro's companies served the docks and
  holds — the two services never mixed. She is a competent, unremarkable
  armswoman, drills without excelling, and documents everything. **Ferro
  does NOT know.** Steward Rooke does NOT know — he is the red herring
  (lore/dynasty/fleet.md). Remember her standing order to protect the
  *Warrant itself* at all costs: in a crisis this may make "Vael" the
  armswoman who saves the Lord-Captain's life.
- **Ferro's unvoiced doubt.** He has reconstructed the duel from servants'
  talk and knows the shape of what he cannot prove: that Vane was goaded, and
  that a sworn lifeward could not lawfully have consented to it
  (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md, GM secrets). He has told no one and acts
  on nothing — his oath forbids it. But the doubt is a fault line: if hard
  proof ever reached him, his honor would tear along it. Renn knows this and
  is patiently gathering exactly that proof.
- **Karsh's blind spot.** She personally vetted every Scion and every line
  recruit — and left the retainer rolls to Ferro as a courtesy of
  jurisdiction. The one formation she never screened is the one that was
  infiltrated. If a hunt for a spy ever starts, her own thoroughness
  elsewhere will point it at the wrong formations first.

## Hooks

1. **The transfer of colors.** The first ground deployment or founding
   ceremony requires Ferro to present the retainers' oath to Aurelian in
   person, before both companies, beside Vane's empty scabbard. How the
   Lord-Captain handles it — honoring the dead man, ignoring him, or
   claiming him — moves the entire retainer bloc, and Renn will record every
   word.
2. **Four kinds of charge pack.** The first sustained action exposes the
   supply chaos: companies cross-drawing incompatible cells under fire.
   Fixing it means salvage (the dead waystation on Hesperus Prime III), trade
   (the quiet ship — lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md), or local
   manufacture; each option drags the regiment deeper into a hook.
3. **The word in the mess.** A line trooper repeats dockside gossip — "the
   Colonel murdered the old champion" — within retainer hearing, and a
   grudge-bout becomes a near-riot on the troop decks. A discipline scene
   that forces Aurelian between Karsh's summary justice and Ferro's household
   law, with the Hunger whispering that leniency looks weak.
4. **Who commands on the ground?** The first opposed landing forces the
   precedence question Karsh and Ferro have been politely not asking. Settle
   it well and the regiment fuses; settle it badly and there are two armies
   on one beachhead.
5. ***Spite* falls over.** The one running Sentinel fails at a dramatic
   moment, and Vex-Han-9's adepts name their price for resurrection — a
   favor tied to the Magos' own obsessions (lore/people/vex_han_9.md) —
   teaching the player early that the Mechanicus contract has interest
   payments.
