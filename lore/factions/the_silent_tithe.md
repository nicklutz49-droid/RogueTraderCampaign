She arrived four months before the Vharadan flotilla, slipping through the
Vortigern Passage in the first desperate wave, and she has spent every day
since among the fourteen moons of Hesperus Prime V, quietly stripping the
richest wreck in the Reach. She has not hailed because silence is her
native tongue — and because she is not yet finished.

## The ship

The *Silent Tithe* was a Havoc-class merchant raider before three
generations of Locke refits made her something narrower and stranger:
half her gun decks torn out for auspex arrays, deep-vox interceptor
farms, and cogitator stacks that would flatter a Navy scout squadron.
She cannot win a stand-up fight against anything her own size and is not
built to try. She is built to *see first, hear everything, and leave* —
her drives are overtuned, her hull-skin baffled against auspex return,
and her machine-spirit is a listener by temperament, sullen when made to
shout.

She has been watching the Vharadan flotilla since translation. Locke's
sensoria read the three ships' silhouettes, drive-signatures, and vox
discipline within hours of arrival. She could recite the *Intemperance*'s
gun count. The flotilla, by contrast, knows only that something quiet sits
near the gas giant. That asymmetry is Locke's favorite weather.

## The crew

Perhaps four thousand souls — small for the tonnage, deliberate. Locke
poaches rather than presses: sensoria adepts lured off chartist hulls,
Navy auspex-men bought out of bad postings, wreck-divers, void-salvors,
lector-savants who read dead registries for a living. Pay is good and
punctual; the unofficial ship's motto is *"the Tithe hears everything and
repeats nothing"* — crew who sell what the ship knows go out an airlock
with their severance in their teeth, and everyone aboard considers this
fair. Culture: quiet decks, low lighting, a near-religious hatred of
unnecessary vox traffic. Her people call salvage work "gleaning" and the
*Lamplighter* site "the Bonefield."

Named crew (minor, coined for color):
- **Mistress of Auspex Ondine Skell** — Locke's chief sensoria savant,
  poached from a Battlefleet Calixis survey tender; half her scalp is
  socket-work. It was Skell who first resolved the *Lamplighter*'s hull
  from the moon-shadow clutter.
- **First Officer Absalom Dree** — dry, elderly, has served three Lockes;
  handles the crew so the Lady-Captain can handle everyone else.
- **Wrackmaster Juno Bell** — chief of the salvage gangs; lost an arm to a
  collapsing bulkhead in the Bonefield's third month and had it replaced
  with a *Lamplighter*-salvaged augmetic she shows off like a wedding ring.

## Lady-Captain Vespasia Locke

House Locke of Malfi holds a genuine Warrant of Trade — minor, ancient,
and whittled thin by centuries of settlements, mortgages, and bad heirs.
Vespasia inherited the vellum, one ship, and the family lesson: *a small
Warrant wins nothing by force; it wins by being first, being informed,
and being legally in the right.* She is somewhere past fifty, Malfian-pale,
unhurried, courteous in the way of someone who has already read your
correspondence. She does not bluff — she considers bluffing a loan against
her own credibility at ruinous interest — but she volunteers nothing, and
she prices everything.

Her methods, in order of preference:

1. **Information.** Know what the other captain wants before the other
   captain says it. The *Silent Tithe*'s arrays exist for this.
2. **Salvage law and claim.** Her Warrant, threadbare as it is, lets her
   register lawful salvage claims and route monopolies; her lector-savants
   draft them beautifully. She would rather serve a rival papers than a
   broadside.
3. **The market.** Sell knowledge to every side of a dispute; remain the
   one party everyone needs alive.
4. **Departure.** If a position cannot be held by the above, it is not
   worth holding. The *Tithe* is faster than anything that wants her dead.

Violence sits last on the list, but it is on the list. Locke has scuttled
one mutiny and outmaneuvered two boarding attempts in her career, and the
freebooters of the Calixis fringe tell those stories, not her.

## The Lamplighter and the Bonefield

Four months ago, working inward from the Passage mouth with pre-Imperial
registry fragments and patience, Locke found what two millennia had
hidden: the wreck of the **Lamplighter**, Sabra Vortigern's survey
flagship, lost with the Vortigern Passage in 743.M39
(lore/places/hesperus_reach.md). The hulk drifts in a trailing cluster of
debris among the gas giant's outer moons — cold, holed, tumbling slowly,
her crew long since mummified at their stations. Something broke her; the
damage reads as neither weapon-fire nor collision, and Locke's savants
have stopped speculating aloud.

Her gangs have gleaned the wreck deck by deck: archeotech auspex
components, the survey vessel's irreplaceable instrument suite, relics
worth a fortune to collectors and the Mechanicus alike. But the true
prize came out of the sealed chart-vault behind the Navigator's sanctum:

**The pre-storm charts.** Partial but authentic survey charts of the
Hesperus Reach as Vortigern mapped it — routes, tide-records, and colony
plots that no living soul possesses. They list Sabra's Rest, Cinderfall,
and Meloria with approach solutions, plus surveyed routes nobody else in
the Reach can even guess at. In a region where the Administratum is
decades away and first Warrant-holders write the rules, these charts are
worth more than any cargo the *Tithe* could lift. Locke knows exactly
what she is holding. So, contractually, would House Vor Kaas — whose
first-claim clause on Passage charts (lore/factions/house_vor_kaas.md)
makes any Vharadan acquisition of Locke's prize instantly complicated.

The salvage is not finished. Weeks of gleaning remain in the deep decks,
and the chart-vault yielded fragments she has not yet collated. This — not
fear — is why she has not hailed.

## What Locke knows (and how garbled it is)

Among the chart-vault fragments are the *Lamplighter*'s final survey
notes, water-damaged, partially vault-burned, in a M39 survey cipher her
savants are still unpicking. What she has so far:

- Vortigern's surveyors logged the geometric ruin at Hesperus Prime IV
  and marked it **"anomalous — auspex-refractory — DO NOT CLOSE"** in the
  flagship's own hand.
- A fragmentary annotation associates the ruin with what the notes call
  *"the stillness"* — the Reach's eerily becalmed warp — and a final,
  incomplete line ties a change in the ruin to the onset of the storms
  that sealed the Passage.

Locke's working conclusion: **the ruin and the Passage storms are
connected, and whoever controls or understands the ruin may hold the
Passage itself.** She does not know how, or why, or what the ruin is
(the truth: lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md — she has perhaps a
tenth of it, garbled). It is the most valuable incomplete sentence in the
Reach, and she treats it accordingly: told to no one, priced at nothing
yet, because she cannot yet name what she'd be selling.

## Partner, pawn, or problem — her read on the flotilla

Locke has spent Day 1 doing what she always does: listening. Three ships,
one of them a bulk transport riding heavy — colonists, therefore a
foothold attempt, therefore a dynasty spending its last throne on the
Reach. A Sword-class with old honors. A raider crewed loud and hungry.
Her assessment of Aurelian Vharadan is pending, but the shape of her
calculus is fixed:

| Reading | What makes it true | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner** | The Vharadans have troops, guns, and colonial weight — everything the *Tithe* lacks. Locke has charts and knowledge — everything they lack. | Approach on her terms: chart access and intelligence traded for protection of her claims and a cut of what the charts unlock. She keeps the originals. Always. |
| **Pawn** | An ambitious Lord-Captain who can be aimed — at louder hooks, at rivals, at the incoming wave (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md) — while she gleans in peace. | Feed the flotilla curated fragments through intermediaries; let Vharadan spend blood mapping what her charts already show, confirming them for free. |
| **Problem** | A usurper's flag, a hungry crew, and a Warrant that grants conquest. If Vharadan moves on the gas giant or asserts claim over the wreck, she is looking at a bigger dog in her yard. | She does not fight. See below. |

She is genuinely undecided, and she is in no hurry — every day of silence
is another day of salvage.

## If ignored — and if threatened (off-screen clock)

**If the flotilla ignores her** and chases the louder hooks (Veyra, the
ruin), Locke finishes the *Lamplighter* at leisure — GM guidance: give
her four to eight weeks of campaign time — then chooses her moment to
make contact as the best-informed actor in the system, holding complete
pre-storm charts and a finished read on the Lord-Captain. The longer she
is left alone, the stronger her opening position. Advance this clock in
state/world_state.md between scenes.

**If the flotilla approaches with respect** — a courteous hail, a parley,
no weapons-lock — she answers. Courtesy is cheap and information about
Aurelian is exactly what she wants. First contact would be voice-only,
precise, and faintly amused.

**If threatened** — weapons-lock, boarding posture, a claim asserted over
the wreck — she does not give battle. In order:

1. **Withdraw and cache.** The *Tithe* breaks off with the charts and her
   best salvage; she can outrun anything Vharadan flies. Prepared caches
   already exist on two of the outer moons.
2. **Serve papers.** A lawful prior-salvage claim on the *Lamplighter*,
   sealed under the Locke Warrant, transmitted wide-band for every
   incoming hull to log. Cheap, permanent, and poisonous to Vharadan
   standing when the Imperium creeps in behind the Passage traffic.
3. **Sell the problem.** Auction her knowledge of the Vharadan
   flotilla — strength, disposition, debts legible from its silhouettes —
   to the next arrivals through the Passage. Baltrane tonnage and worse
   are coming (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md), and Locke brokers for
   whoever pays.
4. **Never** sell the ruin-storm fragment except as a last resort, and
   never complete. It is her deepest reserve.

An enemy who cannot be caught, holds the only maps, and talks to everyone
who arrives next: that is what "problem" looks like from the other side.

## GM secrets

- **The charts are incomplete, and she knows where the rest is.** The
  *Lamplighter*'s vault referenced a second chart-set carried aboard
  Vortigern's escort on the final expedition — a wreck Locke has NOT
  found. Her pre-storm charts have gaps she conceals in every dealing.
  Anyone who buys "the charts" is buying most of them.
- **The Bonefield frightens her crew.** Whatever killed the *Lamplighter*
  left no wreckage of an attacker and no log of a fight — only a final
  entry from Vortigern's Navigator, half-legible: *"…the stillness is not
  empty."* Locke has quietly restricted the deep decks to volunteer gangs
  on double shares. She files this beside the ruin-storm fragment and
  does not look at it late in the ship's night. (The truth she is
  circling: §7.2 deep canon, lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md.
  She must never be given more than garbled tenths of it.)
- **The Warrant is her soft spot.** The Locke Warrant is lawful but
  encumbered — Malfian creditors hold settlements against it that an
  Administratum audit could weaponize. Threats to her ship she can
  outrun; a credible legal threat to the Warrant itself is the one lever
  that genuinely moves her. Usurper's Instinct may let Aurelian smell
  this long before anyone says it aloud.
- **She will recognize the Vor Kaas problem before Aurelian does.** The
  moment she learns a Vor Kaas Navigator steers the flagship, she will
  deduce the first-claim clause on Passage charts and price her originals
  accordingly — or offer them to the Spire Obscura directly, over the
  dynasty's head, if Vharadan plays her wrong.

## Hooks

1. **The hail that isn't.** The flotilla vox-hails the quiet ship and gets
   silence — then, hours later, a tight-beam reply arrives that quotes the
   *Intemperance*'s own internal muster figures back at her. Locke's
   opening move: demonstrate what she knows, concede nothing, invite the
   Lord-Captain to talk like someone who has noticed.
2. **The race to the Bonefield.** Vex-Han-9 or the *Pale Wager*'s
   plunder-hungry crew push for a salvage run at the gas giant. Arriving
   ships find claim-buoys under the Locke seal, salvage gangs aboard the
   wreck — and a fast raider that goes dark the moment they close.
   Aurelian's first choice about her: law, parley, or force.
3. **The chart auction.** Locke offers the pre-storm charts — approach
   solutions for Sabra's Rest, Cinderfall, and Meloria — at a price:
   throne, protection, a stake in a colony, or the flotilla's strength
   spent on something she wants surveyed. House Vor Kaas' first-claim
   clause (lore/factions/house_vor_kaas.md) turns any purchase into a
   three-way negotiation Ilyana cannot ignore.
4. **The incomplete sentence.** If Aurelian earns partner status — or
   pays extraordinarily — Locke shares the garbled ruin-storm fragment:
   the first hint any player-facing source gives that Hesperus Prime IV
   is connected to the Passage itself. This should land as a revelation
   with a price tag.
5. **The broker's revenge.** If threatened and driven off, Locke resurfaces
   weeks later attached to a new arrival from the Passage wave — selling
   Vharadan dispositions, filing salvage grievances at Anchorage Vigil,
   and proving that the cheapest enemy in the Reach was the one hail
   Aurelian never sent.
