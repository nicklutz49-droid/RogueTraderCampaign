# IMPERIAL ACTORS — THE INCOMING WAVE

> **Canon status:** GM reference — not yet revealed in play. State files record what the player knows.

The Vortigern Passage is open, and the Imperium is coming through it. Not as
a fleet, not as a policy — as a *pressure*: freebooters first, then faith,
then industry, then the Omnissiah's servants, then the ledger-men, and at
the last the Inquisition. This is the **Passage traffic** off-screen clock
named in state/world_state.md. It ticks in months, and nothing Aurelian
does can stop it — only decide who arrives to find *what*, and who arrives
owing him.

Two Reach facts govern every arrival (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md):
astropathy through the Passage is muffled to weeks-late whispers, so no
arriving actor is well-informed and none can call home quickly; and the
Administratum cannot project power here for decades. First Warrant-holders
write the rules. Aurelian is, by roughly four months, second
(lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md).

## The clock

Months count from campaign Day 1. Advance between sessions; slide beats
earlier or later as drama demands, except where the seed locks a trigger.

| Months | Who arrives | Tonnage & nature | What they want | Pressure on Vharadan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | Freebooters, chartist scum, desperate prospectors | Dozens of small hulls, ones and twos | Salvage, claims, quick fortunes | Nuisance, opportunity, recruits — and witnesses |
| **4–6** | **Arch-Confessor Jubal Kest**, Missionaria Galaxia | One pilgrim hulk; thousands of zealots and armed frateris | Souls. All of them | Collides with Veyra's Church of the Return (lore/places/hesperus_prime_ii_veyra.md) |
| **6–10** | **Lord-Captain Dagny Baltrane**, House Baltrane | Slow, massive industrial tonnage | Ore, fuel, exclusive extraction rights | Buys or breaks local arrangements — including Aurelian's |
| **8–12** | Mechanicus Explorators — **Archmagos Phaelon Ruuk** | Explorator fleet, Lathe Worlds | Archeotech and xenotech; the ruin | **Sooner if Vex-Han-9 signals him** (lore/people/vex_han_9.md) |
| **Year 1–2** | Administratum surveyors; Battlefleet Calixis picket; agents of **Inquisitor Hadria Veck** | Institutional, permanent | Tithes, order, and the state of Aurelian's soul | The slow creep. The campaign's long-term pressure |

Behind all of it, at the Passage's Calixis-side mouth: **Anchorage Vigil**
(below). Whoever influences the Vigil taxes the whole Reach.

## Months 1–3 — the scum of the rush

The first wave is not the Imperium; it is everyone the Imperium was glad to
see go. Freebooters with letters of marque three owners stale, chartist
captains gambling a hull they don't own, prospector combines packed into
converted ore-scows, void-born salvage clans, at least one plague of
missionary-hucksters selling indulgences for the crossing. Reach hands will
soon call them *passage-rats*. They arrive in ones and twos, under-fueled
and over-armed, and they scatter for the nearest rumor of profit.

**GM use:** texture and consequence. They are hirelings, informants, cheap
auxiliaries, claim-jumpers on the Hesperus belt, and — critically —
*witnesses*: everything Aurelian does in these first months travels back
down the Passage as garbled freebooter gossip, arriving in Calixis just in
time to color every later wave's expectations (and to feed the Displaced
Line's slander mill — lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md). Mercy and
atrocity are both remembered; this wave is who remembers them.

None of them individually threatens a Sword-class frigate. Six of them
under one flag would. Nobody has united them yet.

## Months 4–6 — Arch-Confessor Jubal Kest

A Missionaria Galaxia firebrand of the old, terrible school: one pilgrim
hulk crawling through the Passage with thousands of zealots, penitents, and
armed frateris packed to the bilges, coming to bring the Emperor's light to
the benighted Reach whether the Reach likes it or not. Kest preaches
fire-and-brimstone with genuine, incandescent conviction; he has burned
witches, raised cathedrals from mud, and walked unarmed into greenskin
camps to die and inconveniently failed to. He is not a hypocrite, not a
coward, and not for sale. He *is* persuadable — by faith, by miracle, and
by anyone who can frame facts as providence faster than he frames them as
heresy.

**The collision (the reason he matters):** Hesperus Prime II worships the
**Church of the Return** — the Sky Fathers departed into the heavens and
will return to judge or to save (lore/places/hesperus_prime_ii_veyra.md).
Thirty-eight million souls, already primed to kneel to a starship. Kest
will find this EITHER a miracle of providence — a world that kept faith
through the long dark, awaiting only the Creed's correction — OR a
monstrous heresy to be burned out root and pulpit. **Which one is decided
by handling**, and the handling will likely be Aurelian's: whoever
controls Kest's first contact with Veyra, and the framing of what he
finds, aims the hulk's thousands. Sister Marguerite's fluency in the
Veyran dialect and Dialogous standing make her the natural intermediary
(lore/people/sister_marguerite.md) — with everything that implies.

Frateris under arms are a mob with a blessing: useless in a line of
battle, unstoppable in a crusade, catastrophic in a riot. Kest's
quartermaster-of-pilgrims, **Deacon Hessa Marle**, does his counting and
much of his listening; she is the practical door to him.

## Months 6–10 — Lord-Captain Dagny Baltrane, House Baltrane

House Baltrane holds a Warrant younger than Vharadan's and worth ten times
as much: an industrial strip-mining dynasty out of the Calixis forge-trade,
famous for arriving slowly, tonnage beyond argument, and leaving systems
lighter by their crusts. Dagny Baltrane is its Lord-Captain in the Reach —
methodical, contract-minded, unhurried, and immune to insult. She is not
evil. She is a **glacier with a Warrant**: she buys local arrangements
where buying is cheaper, breaks them where breaking is, and regards both as
the same transaction at different prices.

Her interest: the Hesperus belt, Cinderfall's intact orbital refineries
(lore/places/hesperus_reach.md), and any fuel head at the gas giant. Her
method: an advance factor arrives first — **Factor Juna Skell**, polite,
exact, empowered to offer genuinely fair prices exactly once — followed
months later by tonnage that makes the question moot. Baltrane honors
contracts to the letter, which makes the letter the battlefield: against
her, the Vharadan Warrant's archaic and generous clauses
(lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md) are worth more than the flotilla's guns.
She respects prior claim, documented and defended. She does not respect
intention, sentiment, or being asked nicely.

## Months 8–12 — Archmagos Explorator Phaelon Ruuk

Mechanicus interest in a reopened Reach was inevitable; Ruuk makes it
specific. An Archmagos Explorator of the Lathe Worlds, Disciples of Thule
alignment — the faction that holds humanity's lost technology must be
*recovered and used*, doctrinal caution be damned. An Explorator fleet is a
sovereign power: forge-ships, skitarii maniples, and an authority over
"technological patrimony" that the Mechanicus considers senior to any
Warrant of Trade.

**The trigger is aboard Aurelian's own ship.** Magos Vex-Han-9 keeps a
private encrypted vox-cant channel to Ruuk (lore/people/vex_han_9.md, GM
secrets). If Vex-Han-9 confirms a major find at the Hesperus Prime IV ruin
(lore/places/hesperus_prime_iv_lattice.md), he signals — and Ruuk comes
*early*, moving the clock from "months 8–12" to "as fast as an Explorator
fleet can burn." Ruuk's arrival converts the ruin from Aurelian's secret to
a contested prize, and Vex-Han-9 from expedition asset to a man whose two
loyalties have both arrived in-system.

## Year 1–2 — the long game: quill, picket, and Throne

The institutions arrive last and stay forever.

- **Administratum surveyors** — tithe-assessment teams, cartographic
  missions, claim registrars. Slow, humorless, and dangerous in the only
  way that outlasts everyone: what they *record* becomes what is true. The
  Vharadan Warrant's generous clauses are a lawyer's goldmine — for
  whichever side lawyers first.
- **A Battlefleet Calixis picket** at the Passage mouth — a token squadron
  to show the flag and log traffic. Meaningless as force, decisive as
  *witness*: once the Navy logs who passes, the Reach's deniability ends.
  A live nerve for First Officer Cole, whose settlement with the Admiralty
  binds him to silence (lore/people/brannt_cole.md).
- **Agents of Inquisitor Hadria Veck**, Ordo Hereticus, Calixis Conclave —
  and eventually, if the sermon digests alarm her, **Veck herself**. She
  already has an instrument aboard: Sister Marguerite
  (lore/people/sister_marguerite.md). The muffled astropathy that protects
  Aurelian also starves Veck of data, and an Inquisitor starved of data
  eventually comes to look.

## Anchorage Vigil — the chokepoint

An Imperial way-station being erected at the Calixis-side mouth of the
Passage: dock-spurs, a victualling yard, a Navy provost office, an
Administratum registry desk, and a chapel — the Imperium in miniature,
bolted to the only door into the Reach. Every wave above stops there,
refits there, files there, and gossips there. Its current wharf-master,
**Wharf-Commandant Aldos Pryne** (Navy provost, mid-career, expensively
bribable in small ways and terrified of being caught in large ones), is a
placeholder for the real prize:

**Whoever influences the Vigil taxes the whole Reach.** Berthing
precedence, fuel prices, whose claims get registered first, whose cargoes
get inspected slowest, what news travels down-Passage and in what shape.
This is long-game politics — played by proxy, by tithe, by chapel
endowment, by marriage of convenience between registry clerks and trade
factors. The Displaced Line's legal siege will reach for the Vigil too
(lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md): it is where the Calixis grudge and
the Reach eventually meet. Aurelian cannot hold it with guns. He could own
half of it with foresight.

## Running the clock

Advance per state/world_state.md. Each wave should be *heard before it is
seen*: freebooter gossip precedes Kest; Kest's pilgrims gossip about
Baltrane's tonnage massing at the Vigil; Skell precedes Baltrane; a
Mechanicus survey drone precedes Ruuk. Give the player the weather report,
never the ambush. The waves interact — Kest's zealots despise Baltrane's
strip-mining of "providential" worlds; Baltrane and Ruuk will collide over
Cinderfall's refineries; everyone wants the Vigil — and Aurelian's leverage
is that he alone is already *here*, with months of standing arrangements
each new arrival must buy, break, or bless.

## GM secrets

- **Vao Krell rides the first wave.** The mutineer ringleader whose refugee
  barges Cole saved at the Cyclades has since turned freebooter and burned
  ships Cole couldn't save (lore/people/brannt_cole.md, GM secrets). He is
  a live hook, not a scheduled arrival: if a freebooter flag starts uniting
  the passage-rats, or a burned chartist hulk drifts into an early scene,
  it can be Krell — and Cole's buried history surfaces with him.
- **Kest's verdict on Veyra is genuinely undecided in canon.** Do not
  pre-commit. The miracle/heresy fork is the player's to aim; Marguerite's
  translation choices (and her Hereticus reporting line) sit invisibly on
  the scale. If Veck's circle learns of a planetary cult *before* Kest
  frames it as providence, the Hereticus reads "heresy" by default.
- **Ruuk's true hunger outranks the ruin.** The Disciples of Thule prize
  human archeotech above xenotech — and beneath Veyra's Carven Range lies
  the buried stern of a Dark Age ark with functional archeotech regalia
  (lore/places/hesperus_prime_ii_veyra.md, GM secrets). Nobody incoming
  knows. If the Mechanicus ever learns, the ruin becomes Ruuk's second
  priority and Veyra's sovereignty his first obstacle. An ark is a prize
  the Mechanicus would burn worlds for.
- **Baltrane did her reading.** Skell carries a dossier on the Vharadan
  usurpation, assembled at Anchorage Vigil from Calixis gossip the
  Displaced Line has been salting (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md).
  Baltrane's opening offers will be priced on the assumption that
  Aurelian's Warrant may be legally clouded — an insult, a lever, and a
  warning of how the whole Imperial wave will price him if the coercion
  case fattens.
- **The picket changes Renn's arithmetic.** An Imperial presence able to
  take lawful custody of a Warrant moves the Displaced Line's
  kill-sanction conditions from impossible toward live
  (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md, GM secrets). The Navy picket and
  the Administratum registrars are that presence. The safer the Reach gets,
  the less safe Aurelian is.

## Hooks

1. **The first flag at the door (months 1–3).** A freebooter squadron
   starts "licensing" salvage in the Hesperus belt under a forged letter
   of marque. Aurelian can crush them (reputation as a hard man travels
   down-Passage), charter them (cheap hulls, dirty hands), or out-paper
   them (the Warrant's first legal flex in the Reach).
2. **The hulk on the horizon (months 4–6).** Astropath Rhee catches Kest's
   hymnal broadcast days out. Aurelian holds every card that matters for
   exactly one meeting: does he steer the Arch-Confessor toward Veyra as
   its prophesied herald, quarantine him from the lost world, or spend
   Marguerite as intermediary and hand her the frame?
3. **Skell's single fair offer (months 6–8).** Factor Juna Skell arrives
   ahead of Baltrane's tonnage with an exact, generous, once-only bid for
   the belt and Cinderfall rights. Accepting funds the dynasty for a year;
   refusing means holding claims against a glacier. Either way, the offer
   reveals how much Baltrane already knows.
4. **The Magos's quiet transmission (any time).** Vex-Han-9 confirms a
   find at the ruin and must decide whether to signal Ruuk — or is caught
   preparing to. If the player's crew intercepts the vox-cant burst, the
   clock becomes a bargaining chip; if not, an Explorator fleet arrives
   months early, already knowing what the Anchor is worth.
5. **A berth at the Vigil (slow burn).** Word arrives that berthing
   precedence and claim-registry order at Anchorage Vigil are quietly for
   sale. A modest investment now — an endowed chapel, a retained registrar,
   a favor to Wharf-Commandant Pryne — buys outsized leverage over every
   later arrival. So Locke has already noticed the same thing
   (lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md).
