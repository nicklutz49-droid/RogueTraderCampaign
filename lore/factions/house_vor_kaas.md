# NAVIGATOR HOUSE VOR KAAS — The Steersmen's Wager

> **Canon status:** GM reference — not yet revealed in play. State files record what the player knows.

A minor-to-middling house of the Navis Nobilite, seated in the **Spire
Obscura**, Hive Sibellus, Scintilla (Calixis Sector). For centuries they
have steered other dynasties' ships along other houses' routes and been
paid in other houses' coin. The reopened Vortigern Passage is their one
chance in a generation to change that arithmetic — and they have bet a
granddaughter of the house on it. Her file: lore/people/ilyana_vor_kaas.md.
The debt as the dynasty carries it: state/dynasty_ledger.md.

## The Spire Obscura

The house seat is a smoke-dark tower grafted to the flank of Hive Sibellus,
its upper floors windowless by design — a Navigator house advertises what
it sees by showing nothing. Within: the Novator's court on the crown
floors; the **Occluded Archive**, the house chart-vault, beneath it,
tended by **Chartmaster Ottavio Vor Kaas** (half-blind, wholly exact, has
never once left the vault levels); and below the court, the *shadow
floors* — the cadet branches whose Navigators do the actual steering that
keeps the house solvent. Ilyana was born down there. The court has never
let her forget it, which is one of several reasons she was available to
spend.

House Vor Kaas is old enough for pride and poor enough that the pride has
edges. They hold no route of their own. Every chart in the Occluded
Archive is licensed, leased, or inherited from settlements of other
houses' debts. The greater Navigator houses of Scintilla treat them as a
staffing agency with a bloodline. This is the wound the Passage gamble is
meant to close.

## Novator Dmitir Vor Kaas

Ancient even by Navigator reckoning, and calculating even by Novator
reckoning. Dmitir has ruled the Spire Obscura for longer than most of his
court has been alive, mostly by treating every person, oath, and
catastrophe as an entry in a ledger that only he can read in full. He is
not cruel; cruelty is expenditure without return. He is not kind either.
He is *precise*.

- **His view of the Vharadan venture:** an acceptable-odds wager with a
  capped downside. He priced it carefully: one elevated Secundus, two
  journeyman Tertius kin, and the house's name on an exclusive contract.
- **His view of Aurelian:** a usurper is a man who honors contracts only
  while they profit him — which Dmitir considers the honest baseline of
  all clients, so no offense taken. He countersigned accordingly, with
  clauses.
- **His view of Ilyana:** his great-niece; a real talent from the wrong
  floors; a stake, not an heir. He gave her a sealed directive the night
  before departure (see lore/people/ilyana_vor_kaas.md) and no letter,
  because a letter admits sentiment and sentiment is expenditure.

He is served in worldly matters by **Factotum Arno Ghyle**, the house's
unmutated man-of-business, who traveled to Port Gyre to witness the
contract's sealing and privately reported the Vharadan flotilla as
"three ships, one regiment, and a great deal of nerve."

## The Passage gamble

The arithmetic, as the Novator's court runs it: warp routes are the only
property a Navigator house can truly own. If the Vortigern Passage
becomes a stable trade route, then within a generation every hull bound
for the Hesperus Reach must buy its steersmen and its charts somewhere —
and the house that holds the Passage charts and supplies its steersmen
**rises a full tier**: from staffing agency to route-holder, from the
shadow of the great houses into their company.

The risk is equally simple. The Passage closed once, violently, for two
millennia (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md). If it closes again, the house
loses a Secundus, two Tertius, and its wager-money. Dmitir judged that
survivable. What is not survivable is *someone else* holding the charts —
which is why the contract reads the way it does.

## The contract

Sealed at Port Gyre before departure; the original rides in the Occluded
Archive, the counterpart in the Vharadan strongbox aboard *Intemperance*
(lore/dynasty/fleet.md). Its substance:

| House Vor Kaas provides | House Vharadan owes |
|---|---|
| Navigator Secundus Ilyana Vor Kaas as senior steersman | **Exclusivity:** no Navigator of any other house may serve the Vharadan flotilla or its charter |
| Navigators Tertius Bors Vor Kaas (*Pale Wager*) and Ephrem Vor Kaas (*Dutiful Son*) | **First claim on all Vortigern Passage route charts** — every transit log, tide-record, and correction, original or acquired |
| Deferred fees — the house is paid in claim, not coin, until the venture profits | Standard indemnities for Navigator loss; berth, staff, and spire-quarters befitting rank |

The exclusivity clause cuts both ways and everyone signed knowing it:
the dynasty cannot replace its Navigators without breaching contract, and
the house cannot recall them without abandoning its claim. Three
Navigators steer three ships. There is no fourth. Whoever thinks about
that longest holds the leverage.

## What the house expects

Concretely, in order of priority:

1. **Charts.** Complete, ciphered, duplicated Passage transit records —
   Ilyana already keeps the house duplicates as a matter of course.
2. **Survival of the claim.** The venture need not triumph; it need only
   keep the contract alive and the charts flowing until the route proves
   stable.
3. **Reports.** Regular astropathic accountings — an expectation already
   souring, since the Reach's muffled astropathy means weeks of delay and
   garbled fragments (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md). Dmitir does not yet
   fully appreciate this. When he does, his patience will be tested in a
   currency he dislikes spending.

**If Vharadan succeeds:** the house honors the contract to the letter and
grows loyal in proportion to profit — more Navigators sent, fees
restructured on generous terms, the Spire Obscura's court suddenly full
of people recalling their warm regard for the Lord-Captain. A rising
route-house makes a genuinely powerful long-term ally: charts, steersmen,
and Navis Nobilite standing are things no amount of conquest buys.

**If Vharadan fails:** the sealed directive in Ilyana's chart-chest is
already written — *salvage the route charts and abandon him*. The house
would grieve the fees, keep the charts, and quietly offer route service
to whichever Warrant comes through the Passage next (the wave is coming:
lore/factions/imperial_actors.md). Nothing personal. Ledger entries
rarely are.

## GM secrets

- **The stake is meant to be spendable.** Dmitir's private calculus, known
  in full to no one else: Ilyana's elevation removed a shadow-floor talent
  who had out-steered the court's own grandsons, and the wager pays the
  house *even if she dies* — provided the charts come home. If she returns
  in triumph, the court will have to make room for her, and Dmitir has
  deliberately not decided whether he will let it. He is betting a problem
  and hoping to win an asset.
- **The house reads its own mail.** Bors and Ephrem both write home
  (lore/people/ilyana_vor_kaas.md); Dmitir treats their letters as an
  audit on Ilyana's reports. He does not know that Ilyana reads Ephrem's
  letters first, and she does not know that Dmitir weighs Ephrem's
  homesick scribblings more heavily than her flawless dispatches —
  precisely because the boy is too frightened to shade the truth.
- **The Dowager has come calling.** Before the flotilla departed, brokers
  acting for the displaced Vharadan main line — Dowager Iolanthe's
  legal-financial siege, lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md — made a
  discreet approach at the Spire Obscura to buy the house's claim against
  the dynasty. Dmitir declined without refusing: the claim is worth more
  if the route lives, and a Novator never sells an option he can keep for
  free. The door remains ajar. If the venture stumbles, the house's paper
  could become the Dowager's knife — and House Vor Kaas would consider
  that an ordinary transaction.
- **The charts outrank the client.** Under every clause runs one house
  law: the Occluded Archive comes first. Any crisis that forces a choice
  between Vharadan lives and Passage charts — including Aurelian's life,
  including Ilyana's — has a pre-decided answer on Scintilla. Ilyana
  suspects this. She has never tested whether she is on the protected
  side of the line.

## Hooks

1. **First claim, exercised.** The moment any Reach charts enter Vharadan
   hands — above all Locke's pre-storm charts from the *Lamplighter*
   (lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md) — the contract's first-claim clause
   activates and Ilyana must invoke it. Aurelian can comply, stall, or
   quietly copy first; each choice sets the house's attitude for years.
2. **The garbled query.** Weeks into the expedition, a mangled astropathic
   sending arrives from Scintilla under the Novator's seal: a demand for
   an accounting of the venture's health. Ilyana must draft the reply;
   what she asks the Lord-Captain to let her say — and what he insists she
   omit — is a quiet scene with a long fuse.
3. **Three Navigators, no spares.** Any plan that risks a Navigator (a
   boarding action, the Hesperus Prime IV null-zones, splitting the
   flotilla) risks stranding a ship in the Reach and breaching the
   exclusivity clause besides. The GM should price Navigator risk
   visibly; the crew certainly does.
4. **The Dowager's paper.** If the Calixis grudge clock advances
   (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md), Iolanthe's brokers may return to
   the Spire Obscura and buy the Vor Kaas claim outright — arriving in
   the Reach not as an assassin but as a creditor's writ with the
   Navis Nobilite behind it.
5. **The offer that outbids him.** A later Passage arrival — Baltrane
   tonnage or Kest's pilgrim hulk (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md) —
   offers House Vor Kaas better terms for Reach steersmen. Dmitir cannot
   breach the exclusivity clause, but he can let Aurelian learn of the
   offer and watch what the Lord-Captain pays to keep his only Navigators
   feeling valued.
