# THE DISPLACED LINE — THE MAIN-LINE COUNTERSTROKE

> **Canon status:** GM reference — not yet revealed in play. State files record what the player knows.

The ousted Vharadan main line: what is left of three thousand years of
dynasty after Aurelian took the Warrant in 815.M41 (the usurpation itself:
lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md). They remain on Scintilla, in the stripped
shell of the Eclipse Court, Hive Sibellus — humiliated, half-ruined, and
already moving. This is the **Calixis grudge** off-screen clock named in
state/world_state.md. It ticks slowly, in Calixis, months of muffled
astropathy away — and one piece of it is already aboard the flotilla.

Their motto is the same as Aurelian's, and they mean it the old way:
*"What we hold, we keep."* They no longer hold it. They intend to keep it
anyway.

## Who remains

| Person | Standing | Danger |
|---|---|---|
| **Lord Merovech Vharadan** | The displaced Warrant-holder. Soft, vain, genuinely wounded — he wears the loss like a mourning band and tells the story at other houses' parties | None himself. His grief is Iolanthe's best propaganda: a wronged lord weeping in salons does more work than a writ |
| **Dowager Lady Iolanthe Vharadan** | Merovech's grandmother. The real spine of the main line for sixty years, and the sole author of its response | The whole of it |
| **Casimir Vharadan** | Merovech's brother; the Cold Trade blackmail lever (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md). Doubly ruined and kept sequestered on Iolanthe's orders | A liability she has locked in a drawer — and a witness she may one day have to spend |
| Cousins, dependents, sinecure-holders | Scattered across Sibellus; humiliated; some quietly relieved to be free of the name's expenses | Gossip, mostly. A few hold small paper Iolanthe is quietly collecting |

Merovech weeps; Iolanthe works. When the family gathered in the wreckage of
816.M41 asking *what do we do*, she answered with the only sentence of hers
the servants repeat: "We do what the boy taught us. Slowly."

## Dowager Lady Iolanthe Vharadan

Bitter, patient, resourceful — the last member of the main line who lived
through the tail of the dynasty's competence, and never forgave the
generations after her for losing it. She is past ninety, juvenat-stretched
and economising even on that; she receives visitors in one heated room of
the Eclipse Court and lets them see the dust sheets on the way in. The
poverty is real. The helplessness is theatre.

Her clarity is the family's one remaining asset: she does not hate Aurelian
for taking the Warrant — she hates him for being *right* about them, which
is far colder fuel. She will not race him, duel him, or denounce him. She
will outlive his momentum. Her instrument is the thing the main line still
owns in abundance: standing, patience, and paper.

## The two-pronged response

Both prongs were in motion before the flotilla cleared Port Gyre. Neither
requires the other; together they are a shears.

### Prong one — the legal-financial siege (Calixis)

Slow strangulation by ledger and reputation, run through Iolanthe's man of
business, **Factor Ambrose Kelm** — a Sibellus debt-scrivener who has served
her for thirty years and holds no illusions about being expendable.

- **Buying the paper.** Kelm's proxies are quietly buying Vharadan debts and
  obligations wherever they surface — including instruments from Aurelian's
  own proxy purchases (two of his proxies still hold paper and records; the
  hunt for them is live — lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md, GM secrets).
  Whoever holds the dynasty's paper holds a voice in any Administratum
  proceeding about the dynasty.
- **Blackening the name.** Discreet, deniable, relentless: word placed with
  tithe-magisters, cartel factors, and Navy provisioners that Vharadan
  contracts under the *new* holder are legally clouded and morally worse.
  Merovech's salon grief is the public face of this.
- **The coercion case.** Advocates are assembling a petition that the
  Warrant transfer of 815.M41 was obtained by coercion: the engineered duel
  and Euric Vane's unreleased lifeward oath as its load-bearing stone, the
  Casimir blackmail and the proxy-debt squeeze as its walls
  (lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md, GM secrets). It is not yet filed —
  Iolanthe will not fire her one shot until the evidence is fat. Renn's
  dispatches (below) are the fattening.

**What the siege can and cannot do.** It cannot reach into the Hesperus
Reach for years; the Administratum projects no power there and astropathy
through the Passage is muffled to a whisper (lore/places/hesperus_reach.md).
What it does is poison the ground *behind* Aurelian: his credit in Calixis,
his future Imperial dealings, any return through the Passage, and — as the
Imperial wave arrives (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md) — the sympathies of
every arriving actor who did their reading before transit. The Anchorage
Vigil way-station at the Passage's Calixis mouth is where the siege and the
Reach will eventually meet.

### Prong two — the knife in the retinue

**Lifeward Cassia Renn**, sworn to Iolanthe personally, embarked among
Armsmaster Ferro's retainer companies before departure. Full embarkation
detail — her cover as **Armswoman Cassia Vael** of the Sable Company, among
the late-mustered cousins-of-service — is in lore/dynasty/house_troops.md,
GM secrets. The Harrowgate family, dock-retainers four generations in the
main line's debt, vouched her onto the rolls; they believe they placed a
poor cousin, not a spy.

## Lifeward Cassia Renn

The Lifewards were the main line's sworn bodyguard corps — Euric Vane was
their captain until Aurelian killed him. They guarded the Eclipse Court and
its persons; Ferro's companies served the docks and holds; the two services
never mixed, which is why no retainer aboard knows her face. Renn was
Iolanthe's recruitment, from outside the household, chosen for exactly the
virtues the mission needs: unremarkable, methodical, and capable of waiting
for years without needing to be told twice.

**The mission, in Iolanthe's order of priority:**

1. **Protect the Warrant itself, at all costs.** The vellum, the seals, the
   unbroken chain of lawful custody. The Warrant is the dynasty; Aurelian is
   an occupant. Nothing — including prong two's other objectives, including
   Renn's own life, including *Aurelian's death* — is worth the Warrant
   lost, burned, or seized by a rival power.
2. **Document.** Aurelian's failures, crimes, and heresies, in evidentiary
   detail: dates, witnesses, words. Every entry feeds the coercion case. She
   documents everything and has since Port Gyre.
3. **Kill — under sanction only.** See below.

**The kill-sanction.** Renn may kill Aurelian ONLY if the Warrant can
afterward pass lawfully back to the main line. In practice that means all
of the following, and she knows her catechism cold: the death must be
unattributable to the main line (a murder taints the claim it serves); the
Warrant's physical custody must be securable in the same hour; and there
must be no rival succession in the way — no designated heir, no Imperial
seizure pending, no circumstance where the Warrant would pass to the
Administratum, a creditor, or another dynasty instead. As of Day 1, in an
unopened Reach crawling with rivals and no lawful chain home, *none* of
these conditions hold. Iolanthe knows this. The sanction is a door she
built for a year that has not come.

**The irony (play this).** Order 1 outranks everything — and Aurelian
carries the Warrant on his person (state/character_sheet.md). If the
Lord-Captain dies wrongly — in a boarding action, to Locke or Baltrane or
a xenos ruin — the Warrant is loose in the Reach, salvage for whoever
stands nearest, and the main line's claim dies with him. Until the
conditions for a lawful reversion exist, the safest place for the Warrant
is a living Aurelian's keeping. Renn has run this logic to its end:
**"Vael" may be the armswoman who saves the Lord-Captain's life**, coldly,
correctly, and under orders — and hate every second of it.

**Discipline and tradecraft.** She drills without excelling, is liked
without being known, and volunteers for nothing except duties that rotate
her near documents and doors. Her records are kept in a memorised private
cipher, dispersed and innocuous. She cannot report home quickly — muffled
astropathy cuts both ways — so she stockpiles, and queues rare dispatches
disguised as a dutiful cousin's letters to the Harrowgates. Iolanthe
expects years of silence and planned for it.

**Who does not know:** **Ferro does NOT know.** He vouched for the
cousin-tradition, not the individuals, and his honor would tear along the
fault of it (lore/dynasty/house_troops.md, GM secrets). **Rooke does NOT
know** — the old steward is a red herring, and a hunt for the main line's
agent will find his forty years of main-line service first
(lore/dynasty/fleet.md, GM secrets). Nobody aboard knows. Renn reports to
Iolanthe alone.

## Running the clock

Advance between sessions, per state/world_state.md. Suggested beats, months
apart (Calixis time, arriving in the Reach garbled and late): Kelm's paper
purchases bite a Vharadan proxy → a cartel quietly delists Vharadan
contracts → the coercion petition is drafted, awaiting evidence → Iolanthe
gains or loses a powerful listener (an Administratum prefect; or, worse for
everyone, Inquisitor Veck's circle — lore/people/sister_marguerite.md, whose
Hereticus interest in the usurpation runs on adjacent rails). The clock
never needs to hurry: it is the reason Aurelian can never simply go home.

## GM secrets

- **Iolanthe's true endgame is the Warrant, not revenge.** If Aurelian were
  ever to offer reconciliation — a main-line heir adopted into succession, a
  Calixis stewardship for Merovech, honors for Vane's memory — she would
  take the deal and fold both prongs the same day, and consider it victory.
  She would also never trust him, and keep Renn in place. Her price is the
  name's continuity; his corpse is merely one acceptable currency.
- **Renn's private fracture.** Euric Vane trained her. She joined the
  Lifewards under his captaincy, and his unlawful death is her private
  reason where Iolanthe's writ is her public one. If she ever obtains hard
  proof of the duel's illegality (the proof Ferro cannot let himself seek —
  lore/dynasty/house_troops.md, GM secrets), her discipline meets its one
  temptation: deliver it to the case as ordered, or put it in front of
  Ferro and break the retainer companies against Aurelian directly. The
  second is off-sanction. She thinks about it anyway.
- **The sanction can mature.** GM lever: any development that creates a
  lawful reversion path — Aurelian formally naming a successor, an
  Administratum or Inquisitorial presence able to take custody
  (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md), a return transit to Calixis — moves
  the kill-conditions from impossible toward live. The player can render
  the knife harmless or lethal without ever knowing it exists.
- **Kelm is the loose thread.** The factor's proxy web is deniable but not
  invisible; anyone auditing who is buying Vharadan paper (see
  lore/dynasty/house_vharadan.md, Hooks) finds Kelm in three steps and
  Iolanthe in four. She knows, and has already written him off.

## Hooks

1. **Garbled word from Scintilla.** Weeks in, Astropath Rhee delivers a
   partial sending from a Calixis contact: Vharadan paper is being bought
   in quantity, buyer unknown; a petition is rumored at the Administratum.
   The siege arrives on-screen as static and dread — nothing Aurelian can
   act on from here, which is the point.
2. **The armswoman at the door.** In a boarding action, ambush, or riot,
   an unremarkable Sable Company armswoman is inexplicably the one who
   gets the Lord-Captain out alive. Commendation puts "Vael" near
   Aurelian's person — exactly where her orders want her, exactly where
   discovery becomes catastrophic.
3. **The letters to the Harrowgates.** Any audit of outbound astropathic
   traffic (the same sweep that threatens Marguerite's sermon digests —
   lore/people/sister_marguerite.md, Hooks) surfaces a trooper's dutiful
   family letters, faintly too regular. Pulled gently, they read as
   homesickness. Pulled hard, prong two detonates aboard ship.
4. **The wrong spy.** Evidence of *an* agent — a copied document, a
   cipher fragment — starts a hunt that runs straight at Rooke (forty
   years' main-line service, acid memoranda) while Karsh's vetting points
   away from the one formation she never screened. Aurelian's Usurper's
   Instinct deserves a fair chance to notice the hunt is too easy.
5. **An emissary through the Passage.** Months in, a chartist or
   freebooter of the early wave (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md)
   carries a sealed offer over Merovech's signature — Iolanthe's words —
   proposing terms. Whether Aurelian burns it, bargains, or reads it
   aloud to his officers, the main line learns something; and Renn, in
   the honor guard, records his answer.
