## Navy career — the rise

Cole came up the hard ladder — signals midshipman, gunnery lieutenant,
executive officer postings where competence mattered more than name. No
naval dynasty, no patron, no margin for error; he made none. *"Cole's watch
never slips,"* went the Battlefleet saying. By 809.M41 he was First Officer
of the *Indefatigable Wrath* under **Captain Hektor Vale** — decent, tired,
one commission from retirement — and expected a cruiser of his own within
five years. Then came the Cyclades Reprisal, and the Navy erased him.

## The fall, as the flotilla knows it

Aboard the *Intemperance* it is common knowledge the First Officer was
cashiered, and common sport to guess why: gambling debts, a duel over a
captain's wife, striking a flag officer, cowardice at the Cyclades — that
last never said where he can hear it. Cole confirms nothing, denies
nothing, disciplines no one. The record, if pulled, shows only: *commission
resigned 810.M41; particulars sealed by order of the Admiralty court.*

## The wilderness years (810–816.M41)

Six years beached: sailing-master and mate on Chartist hulls out of the
Drusus Marches, each berth smaller than the last — a sealed Admiralty
record frightens shipowners worse than an honest crime would. He kept Navy
hours anyway. He kept his cutlass too, though the presentation plaque has
been pried off the scabbard; he has never said what it commemorated.

## Port Gyre — the Vharadan commission

Aurelian Vharadan, fitting out a flotilla in 816.M41 with more Warrant than
treasury, needed a first officer who could make a merchant-house crew fight
like Navy — and could be had cheap. Cole was both. At the interview
Aurelian asked one question about the sealed record: *"Will your reasons
follow us?"* Cole said, *"No, lord."* Aurelian noted the evasion, decided
the competence was worth the mystery, and has not asked since. **He does
not know the truth** (locked in lore/dynasty/fleet.md). Since Port Gyre,
Cole has ground the crew toward Battlefleet drill standards, hired
Navy-pattern hands to stiffen the watches, and stood the Passage transit's
Geller alarms without letting the bridge see him afraid. The old void-born
families call him "the Iron Watch." It is not entirely a compliment. It is
entirely accurate.

## The man

Cole's religion is competence — the one thing that never betrayed him —
practiced like an Ecclesiarchy devotion: gunnery rites timed to the second,
damage-control catechisms in darkened compartments, watch handovers formal
enough for a coronation. One glass of amasec at the end of the last watch,
never before, often with Astropath Rhee, whose dry humour is the nearest
thing he permits himself to friendship.

## Why he respects Aurelian

Cole once broke his oath for principle and lost everything he was. Aurelian
broke half the customs of Calixis society for ambition — and *kept* what he
took. That fascinates Cole the way a perfect enemy manoeuvre fascinates a
gunnery officer. He watches the Lord-Captain like scripture, hunting the
answer to the only question that matters to him: **is it possible to do
the necessary thing and survive it — or did I fail at the Cyclades because
I flinched from going far enough?** His loyalty is real, and conditional in
a way no one suspects: it is loyalty to Aurelian's *competence*. Should the
Lord-Captain ever give the one kind of order Cole cannot execute (see
below), the finest officer in the flotilla becomes its most dangerous man.

## GM secrets

### The Cyclades Reprisal, 809.M41 — what actually happened

Battlefleet Calixis moved to suppress the Cyclades mutinies; the
*Indefatigable Wrath* was in the line. In the reprisal's last hours the
mutineers' remnant broke for open void, screening their run with eleven
refugee evacuation barges — perhaps thirty thousand souls, dockworkers'
families and station crews, lashed into the formation as living armour.
The admiral commanding signalled a simple order: fire. Captain Vale
relayed it; his voice was flat and his hands were not.

Cole drew his service pistol on the captain's chair and announced, in the
tone he used for watch handovers, that the *Wrath* would hold fire. **Chief
Provost Ena Skoll**, master-at-arms of the watch, had her weapon
half-drawn — then holstered it, acquitting every other soul on the bridge
of having to choose. For eleven minutes Cole held the bridge of an Imperial
cruiser at gunpoint while the firing solutions went stale, and the barges —
hidden among them the mutineer ringleader **Vao Krell** — crossed the line
the guns could not be brought back across. He handed Vale his pistol, grip
first, and confined himself to quarters without being asked.

### The quiet court-martial and the settlement

The Admiralty's problem: trying Cole publicly for mutiny meant entering
into evidence *the order itself* — an admiral commanding fire on Imperial
refugee barges — before advocates, clerks, and the Ministorum. The court
convened in camera, 810.M41. The settlement: the capital charge of mutiny
was recorded, proven, and **held in abeyance**; Cole was convicted instead
of gross insubordination, stripped of commission, pension, and honours, and
cashiered. In exchange he signed articles of silence: if he ever speaks of
the order, the admiral, or the proceedings, the abeyance ends and the
mutiny conviction executes. **His silence is enforced by a live death
sentence.** Canon does not name the admiral — the settlement buried the
name; the GM may supply one if play demands. Skoll and the other witnesses
were scattered under the same articles; none serve in the flotilla.

### Vao Krell — the live hook

Krell drew the correct lesson from the Cyclades: *Imperial officers can be
made to blink, if you feed enough innocents into the firing solution.* He
has spent eight years as a freebooter proving it. His raider, the
***Unrepentant***, fights with captured crews lashed into barge-hulks towed
at her flanks, and pursuit after pursuit has broken off rather than burn
them. He has taken ships, torched a Chartist convoy, burned an orbital
dock — always behind his shield of the living. He toasts "the officer of
the *Wrath*" as his patron saint; canon leaves open whether he ever learned
that officer's name — the GM chooses when the reveal serves best. Krell
**may surface in the Reach**: he is exactly the breed riding the first
freebooter wave through the Passage (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md,
months 1–3). Nothing is written as having happened — but the Passage is
open, and the Reach is lawless water.

### The arithmetic

Cole half-believes he was wrong, and keeps the proof like a wound he will
not let close: a private ledger of every hull, dock, and soul credibly
attributed to Krell, hand-written in Navy signals shorthand and locked in
his sea chest — the most incriminating object aboard the flagship. He
reckons the count near nineteen thousand. Eleven barges held perhaps thirty
thousand. The day Krell's tally passes the souls the barges carried, Cole's
arithmetic says the admiral was right — and Cole murdered by mercy every
person Krell has killed since. What he does on that day, canon does not fix.

### Orders he will not execute

As locked in lore/dynasty/fleet.md: there exist orders Cole cannot obey —
deliberate fire on civilian craft, hostages treated as acceptable losses,
anything that is the Cyclades again. Nobody in the flotilla knows this,
including Aurelian. Cole himself does not know which way he would break a
second time; the uncertainty keeps him awake through the middle watches.

### How the secret should surface — a ladder, not a dump

1. **Tremor:** the first order that smells of the Cyclades — civilians in a
   firing solution — Cole requests in writing, or executes a beat too slow.
   Small, deniable, noticed.
2. **Rumour:** incoming Passage traffic carries Navy-touched crews;
   somebody recognizes "the *Wrath*'s old First." Vespasia Locke
   (lore/factions/the_silent_tithe.md) *may*, at the GM's option, hold the
   garbled rumour-version; Lifeward Renn (lore/factions/the_displaced_line.md)
   logs any observed disobedience; Marguerite's Inquisitorial channel could
   unseal the record itself — slowly, given the muffled astropathy.
3. **The man himself:** Krell in the Reach, burning something behind his
   barge-shields. Cole asking leave to hunt him — having to explain, or lie
   about, how he knows the *Unrepentant*'s doctrine — is the strongest
   reveal. Play it as confession under pressure, not exposition.

## Hooks

1. **In writing, lord.** Aurelian orders a boarding or bombardment with
   civilians in the equation; Cole, for the first time, requests the order
   over the Lord-Captain's seal. Usurper's Instinct feels the crack.
2. **Smoke with a signature.** Early freebooter predation leaves wrecks
   with barge-hulks cut loose and spent. Cole identifies the doctrine from
   auspex returns alone, flatly refuses to say how, and is *right*.
3. **The old hand's salute.** A newly arrived crew (freebooter, Chartist,
   later a House Baltrane officer) includes a Cyclades veteran who salutes
   Cole with pointed formality and starts the rumour mill turning aboard.
4. **The hunt he cannot ask for.** Word confirms the *Unrepentant* in the
   Reach. Cole wants Krell dead more than anything in eight years — and
   cannot explain why without dying by settlement. Watch him steer the
   flotilla toward it sideways.
5. **The Navy returns.** When the Battlefleet picket reaches the Passage
   mouth (lore/factions/imperial_actors.md, year 1–2), the settlement's
   keepers are back in vox range. A quiet signal invites Cole to render
   "assistance" — the abeyance remembers him, and it has a use for him.
